ENGINEERING - CAREER GUIDE

## Mechanical Engineer
Mechanical Engineers design, analyze, and test mechanical systems and
products - anything from vehicle components to manufacturing equipment.
Core skills: CAD software (SolidWorks, CATIA, AutoCAD), understanding of
materials and manufacturing processes, simulation/analysis tools (ANSYS,
FEA), and strong fundamentals in physics and thermodynamics.
Common tools: CAD software, simulation/FEA tools, MATLAB/Simulink for
modeling, and increasingly Python for data analysis and automation of
engineering workflows.
To grow: build a portfolio of real design projects (even personal/academic
ones) that show the full process from concept to analysis to iteration.
Learning to pair traditional mechanical design skills with basic
programming/scripting (e.g. automating repetitive analysis tasks) is
increasingly valuable and differentiates candidates in a competitive field.
Certifications like FE (Fundamentals of Engineering) and later PE
(Professional Engineer) matter more in some industries (civil-adjacent,
regulated industries) than others.

## Systems/Design Engineer
Systems and Design Engineers focus on how individual components fit into
a larger system - requirements definition, interface analysis, and
ensuring subsystems work together correctly (common in automotive,
aerospace, and complex product development).
Core skills: requirements engineering, systems thinking, cross-functional
communication (working with mechanical, electrical, and software teams),
and documentation (architectural diagrams, requirement traceability).
To grow: experience working across multiple engineering disciplines
(not just one) is valuable here, since the role is fundamentally about
integration. Building experience with formal requirements/verification
processes (common in regulated industries like automotive or aerospace)
helps significantly for more senior systems engineering roles.

## Manufacturing/Process Engineer
Manufacturing Engineers design and improve the processes used to
produce products - focusing on efficiency, quality, and cost.
Core skills: process design and optimization, quality tools (Six Sigma,
DFMEA), understanding of manufacturing equipment/automation, and basic
statistics for process control.
To grow: a Six Sigma certification (Green Belt or higher) is highly
valued in this field and demonstrates structured process-improvement
skill. Hands-on plant floor experience, even early in a career, builds
credibility that's hard to get purely from classroom knowledge.
