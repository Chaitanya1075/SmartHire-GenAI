FINANCE - CAREER GUIDE

## Financial Analyst
Financial Analysts evaluate financial data to guide business decisions - things
like budgeting, forecasting, and investment analysis.
Core skills: strong Excel skills (financial modeling, pivot tables), understanding
of financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow), and
increasingly, basic knowledge of a BI tool (Power BI, Tableau) or SQL for
pulling data directly.
Common tools: Excel, financial modeling templates, ERP systems (SAP, Oracle),
and reporting/BI dashboards.
To grow: build strong Excel modeling skills first - this is the single most
requested skill for this role. Learning SQL and a BI tool sets you apart, since
many analysts still rely on manually exported spreadsheets. Certifications
like CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst) are valuable for more senior/investment
focused roles but are a long-term commitment, not needed for entry-level roles.

## Accountant
Accountants track and record an organization's financial transactions, ensure
compliance with regulations, and prepare financial statements and tax filings.
Core skills: bookkeeping fundamentals, understanding of GAAP/accounting
standards, attention to detail, and comfort with accounting software.
Common tools: QuickBooks, Tally, SAP, Excel, and tax preparation software.
To grow: a professional certification (CPA in the US, CA in India, or
equivalent) significantly increases career ceiling and salary. Early career,
focus on mastering one accounting software thoroughly and understanding the
full month-end/quarter-end close process, since that's core to almost every
accounting job.

## Investment Analyst
Investment Analysts research companies, markets, and securities to support
investment decisions, often within an investment bank, asset management firm,
or private equity fund.
Core skills: financial modeling and valuation (DCF, comparable company
analysis), strong understanding of markets, and the ability to write clear
investment research/recommendations.
Common tools: Excel (advanced modeling), Bloomberg Terminal, and increasingly
Python for quantitative analysis.
To grow: this is a competitive, often networking-driven field. Build a
portfolio of practice financial models, follow markets closely and be able
to discuss investment theses, and target internships early since this field
weighs relevant experience heavily.
