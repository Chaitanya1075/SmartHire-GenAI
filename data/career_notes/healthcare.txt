HEALTHCARE - CAREER GUIDE

## Registered Nurse / Clinical Staff
Registered Nurses and other clinical staff provide direct patient care -
monitoring patients, administering treatments/medications, and coordinating
with doctors and other care team members.
Core skills: clinical knowledge relevant to the care setting, strong
attention to detail (medication accuracy, documentation), communication
with patients and families, and the ability to stay calm under pressure.
Common requirements: a nursing license (RN licensure, which requires
completing an accredited nursing program and passing a licensing exam),
and often certifications specific to a specialty (e.g. critical care,
pediatrics).
To grow: specializing in a high-demand clinical area (ICU, ER, surgical)
often increases both pay and opportunities. Pursuing further education
(e.g. Nurse Practitioner) opens up more autonomous clinical roles with
higher earning potential, though it requires additional schooling.

## Healthcare Administrator
Healthcare Administrators manage the business and operational side of
healthcare facilities - staffing, budgets, compliance, and coordinating
between clinical staff and organizational leadership.
Core skills: healthcare regulations/compliance knowledge (e.g. HIPAA),
budgeting and operations management, and the ability to communicate well
with both clinical staff and non-clinical leadership.
Common tools: healthcare management systems, scheduling software, and
standard budgeting/reporting tools.
To grow: a degree in Healthcare Administration or an MBA with a healthcare
focus helps for more senior roles. Building experience in a specific
functional area first (e.g. billing/compliance, then operations) is a
common path toward broader administrative leadership roles.
