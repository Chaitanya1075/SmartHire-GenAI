HUMAN RESOURCES - CAREER GUIDE

## HR Generalist
HR Generalists handle a broad mix of HR functions - onboarding, employee
relations, policy administration, benefits coordination, and often light
recruiting - especially in small-to-mid sized companies.
Core skills: strong communication, knowledge of basic labor law/compliance,
conflict resolution, and organizational skills to juggle many different
employee-facing tasks at once.
Common tools: HRIS systems (Workday, BambooHR, SAP SuccessFactors), payroll
software, and standard office tools.
To grow: HR Generalist is often a stepping stone toward either HR Management
(people leadership) or a specialized track (recruiting, compensation &
benefits, L&D). Building visible expertise in one area (e.g. becoming the
go-to person for onboarding or benefits) helps with a specialization move.
An HR certification (SHRM-CP or similar) helps credibility, especially for
those without a formal HR degree.

## Recruiter
Recruiters source, screen, and manage the hiring pipeline for open roles,
working closely with hiring managers to fill positions.
Core skills: sourcing techniques (LinkedIn, job boards, referrals), strong
interviewing/screening ability, negotiation (especially for offers), and
the ability to manage many candidates/roles simultaneously without dropping
the ball.
Common tools: Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) like Greenhouse or Lever,
LinkedIn Recruiter, and scheduling tools.
To grow: specializing (technical recruiting, executive search, campus
recruiting) often leads to higher pay and more interesting work than staying
generalist. Building a strong network and reputation for placing good
candidates matters more here than almost any certification.

## HR Manager
HR Managers oversee HR strategy and a team of HR staff, often owning
policy decisions, performance management systems, and being a key partner
to senior leadership on people matters.
Core skills: people leadership, strategic thinking (aligning HR practices
to business goals), deep knowledge of employment law/compliance, and
strong stakeholder management with leadership.
To grow: this role typically requires several years of HR Generalist or
specialist experience first. Building experience managing a team, leading
a policy or culture initiative, and developing comfort discussing HR
metrics/ROI with leadership all help make this transition.
