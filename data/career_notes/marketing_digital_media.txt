MARKETING & DIGITAL MEDIA - CAREER GUIDE

## Marketing Coordinator / Manager
Marketing Coordinators and Managers plan and execute campaigns to promote
a company's products, services, or brand - across channels like social
media, email, content, and paid advertising.
Core skills: campaign planning, content creation/copywriting, basic
analytics (understanding what's working and why), and familiarity with
marketing platforms.
Common tools: social media platforms and schedulers (Hootsuite, Buffer),
email marketing tools (Mailchimp, HubSpot), Google Analytics, and design
tools (Canva, or Adobe Creative Suite for more design-heavy roles).
To grow: build a portfolio of real campaigns you've run, even small ones,
and be able to talk about actual results (engagement, leads generated,
growth numbers) - this is a field where a portfolio matters more than
almost any other credential. Learning basic data analysis (Google
Analytics, or even just Excel) sets marketers apart, since so much of
modern marketing is data-informed.

## Digital Media Specialist
Digital Media Specialists focus specifically on digital channels - social
media, video content, website content, and online advertising - creating
and managing a brand's digital presence.
Core skills: content creation (video, graphics, or writing depending on
specialization), platform-specific knowledge (what works on Instagram vs
LinkedIn vs YouTube), and basic performance tracking.
Common tools: video/photo editing tools (Adobe Premiere, Photoshop,
Canva), social media management platforms, and analytics dashboards for
each platform.
To grow: specializing in one content format (video editing, short-form
content, or a specific platform) and building a visible portfolio (a
personal Instagram/YouTube/portfolio site showing real work) is often
more valuable early on than a broad but shallow skill set. Staying
current with platform algorithm/format changes is a constant, ongoing
part of this career.

## Public Relations / Communications
PR and Communications professionals manage how an organization is
perceived publicly - media relations, press releases, crisis
communication, and often internal communications too.
Core skills: strong writing (press releases, statements, talking points),
relationship-building with media contacts, and calm, clear judgment
under pressure (especially for crisis communications).
To grow: building real media relationships and a portfolio of coverage
you've helped secure is the strongest signal in this field. Internships
at agencies or in-house PR teams are a common and valuable entry point,
since so much of the job depends on trust and existing relationships
that take time to build.
